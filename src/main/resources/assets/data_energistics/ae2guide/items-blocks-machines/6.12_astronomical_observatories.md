The two observation facilities convert AE energy into **Stellar Flux**. Stellar Flux is an AE key stored by the
connected network; it is not the network's AE power buffer.

Only the **Astronomical Observatory** and **Interference Array Core** connect to the AE network and produce on ticks.
The observation-mirror center stores the core's exclusive claim; mirror panels and Celestial Waveguides are structural
blocks with no independent machine, buffer, or production logic.

## Astronomical Observatory

<Row>
  <ItemImage id="data_energistics:astronomical_observatory" scale="6" />
  <RecipeFor id="data_energistics:astronomical_observatory" />
</Row>

The single-block observatory must be connected to an active AE network and have an unobstructed sky directly above its
center. In ordinary day/night dimensions it observes from tick **13,000** (inclusive) through tick **23,000**
(exclusive). Clear weather produces 8 Stellar Flux/t for 4,000 AE/t; rain produces 25% of that output, while
thunder stops the machine. If either the destination storage or AE power is insufficient, the tick is skipped without
spending a partial amount.

## Interference Array Core

<Row>
  <ItemImage id="data_energistics:interference_array_core" scale="6" />
  <RecipeFor id="data_energistics:interference_array_core" />
</Row>

The core is the bottom-center controller of a **5x5x3** base. Fill every other base position with Data Framework, and
place a Celestial Waveguide at the middle of each side on the second layer. The core's lower face is the AE network
port.

## Observation mirror unit

<Row>
  <ItemImage id="data_energistics:astronomical_mirror" scale="6" />
  <RecipeFor id="data_energistics:astronomical_mirror" />
  <ItemImage id="data_energistics:astronomical_mirror_panel" scale="6" />
  <RecipeFor id="data_energistics:astronomical_mirror_panel" />
</Row>

Each unit is one center mirror surrounded by eight mirror panels, forming a 3x3 aperture. Every one of those nine
blocks must see the sky. A unit must be within 32 horizontal blocks and 4 vertical blocks of its core and may be
connected by a Celestial Waveguide path no longer than 32 blocks. A mirror is exclusively claimed by one core.

The core calculates array output from the number of valid mirrors it has claimed: mirrors 1–4 each contribute 40 Stellar
Flux/t, and later mirrors use the configured diminishing tiers. The core consumes 25,000 AE/t plus 10,000 AE/t for every
valid mirror. Fewer than four valid mirrors stops the array; rain applies the same output multiplier as the single-block
observatory and thunder stops it.

All dimension tags, observation windows, production multipliers and energy costs are server configuration/data-pack
rules. The three blocks below are orbital weapon endpoints and controls; they do not produce Stellar Flux.

## Orbital control console

<GameScene zoom="5" background="transparent" interactive={true}>
  <Block id="data_energistics:orbital_control_console" x="0" y="0" z="0" />
  <IsometricCamera yaw="205" pitch="25" />
</GameScene>

<Row>
  <ItemImage id="data_energistics:orbital_control_console" scale="6" />
  <RecipeFor id="data_energistics:orbital_control_console" />
</Row>

Placing a console creates and binds one orbital weapon for the placing player. Right-clicking it opens the server
fire-control interface. The console is an AE network endpoint and remains linked to that weapon until the block is
removed.

An uplink beacon is an additional endpoint for an existing weapon owned by the placing player; it does not create a
weapon by itself. It extends the weapon's online control and projection presence into the beacon's dimension.
