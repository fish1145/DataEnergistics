# Data Distribution Tower

> Chuunibyou quote: Using data as the measure, this tower will calmly and strictly redistribute connections.

The Data Distribution Tower takes over controllerless AE subnets through **virtual allocation** and equalizes FE endpoints by capacity ratio. The tower and every target retain distinct Grid identities: no physical IGridConnection is created and no Grid is merged.

Placement requires **3 blocks** of clear vertical space above the tower base.

The base must be connected to an ME network. The tower is an ordinary REQUIRE_CHANNEL device and consumes one real physical channel; virtual devices are not capped by the 8/32-channel cable feeding the tower.

- Right-click any part of the tower body barehanded: Open the control interface.
- Stealth bare-handed right-click: Switch Range Display.

### Range Adjustment

- **Point-to-Point Connection**: Takes over anchors selected with a Distribution Connector. Bindings remain persistent even if the range is changed later.
- **Range Connection**: Continuously discovers only disconnected single-node Grids in range. Multi-node or physically wired targets are reported as conflicts.

### Connection Targets

- **AE only**: Establish channel connections only for devices with available AE nodes.
- **FE only**: Only devices capable of inputting or outputting FE.
- **AE and FE**: Enable both of these features simultaneously.

Point mode resolves the AE Capability exposed on all six anchor faces and de-duplicates node identities. One anchor may expose several subnets. Valid controllerless subnets are accepted while Controller grids, grids owned by another primary, and bridge cycles are skipped, so a bind may partially succeed. Only the anchor must share the tower dimension and be in bind range; members of an accepted subnet may be out of range or cross-dimensional.

The subordinate subnet keeps its own Pathing service while all other Grid services delegate to the primary. Each REQUIRE_CHANNEL node requests one virtual lease; channel-free nodes cost nothing. A node without a lease stays offline without preventing sibling nodes from coming online. Disabling a device releases only that device's lease and FE participation.

Capacity follows the Controller count: controller conflict supplies 0; a controllerless primary supplies “32 × ChannelMode”; every native Controller contributes all six faces, including faces adjoining another Controller. A positive per-face capacity reported by a Controller node replaces the native 32-channel fallback, while controller types that publish an authoritative controller-wide supply use that total directly. Infinite mode is unlimited. Native AE2 Pathing allocates physical channels first, and virtual capacity is “total minus physical usage.” Manual bindings precede automatic bindings, then first application wins FIFO; shrinking capacity revokes in reverse order.

## Distribution Connector

<Row>
  <ItemImage id="data_energistics:data_distribution_connector" scale="6" />
  <RecipeFor id="data_energistics:data_distribution_connector" />
</Row>

The Distribution Connector is used only in **Point-to-Point Connection** mode:

1. Hold the Distribution Connector, sneak right-click Data Distribution Tower, and bind the connector to the tower.
2. Within the same dimension and within the equalizing tower's range, right-click the AE or FE device you want to connect.
3. After a successful connection, the target will appear in the device list on the equalizing tower control interface.

When you place an already bound connector in the offhand or dedicated trinket slot, newly placed compatible devices will also automatically try to connect to that tower.

> The anchor must share the tower dimension, be in range, and expose either an AE node or any FE input/output capability. Source-only FE devices are valid. A connector cannot directly bind a cross-dimensional anchor, but cross-dimensional members of its subnet participate normally.

## FE energy distribution

All active towers on one primary Grid share one cross-dimensional endpoint snapshot and one equalization transaction. Endpoints include tower coverage, manual and automatic bindings, loaded subordinate members, and Applied Flux. Every capability query first checks that the chunk is loaded and never force-loads it.

Balanced machine endpoints target a common **capacity fill ratio**, not an equal FE amount. Source-only endpoints supply first, sink-only endpoints receive only, and ordinary bidirectional endpoints move only the amount above or below the equalization line. The Applied Flux network is a bidirectional buffer instead of a proportional-capacity endpoint: after ordinary sources, it fills the machines' remaining real receivable gaps, and it absorbs ordinary source surplus. All extraction and insertion is frozen and simulated before execution; an already balanced state causes no real mutation. Short writes compensate sources, and uncompensated FE enters the tower's quarantine buffer with an error log.

When Modern Industrialization is installed, sided MI EU storages participate directly. Conversion uses MI's current `forgeEnergyPerEu` server setting, and FE remainders smaller than one EU remain unmoved during preflight.

## Control Interface

The header shows total capacity, physical usage, virtual leases, and remaining capacity. Each subordinate node has its own row with “Allocated,” “Waiting for channel,” “Disabled,” “Waiting for target,” “Conflict,” or “Bridge error,” plus requested/granted channels, failure reason, and frozen FE state.

- 'Shift + Right-click' target: Turn off or restore the transmission of this target.
- 'Middle mouse button' positioned target: Highlights it and displays coordinates.
- 'Shift + Middle Mouse Button' positioned target: Administrators can teleport directly to it.

Logical nodes without coordinates are grouped at their binding anchor. They can be disabled individually but cannot be highlighted or teleported to.

## Scope of Effect

<Row>
  <ItemLink id="ae2:wireless_booster" />
  <ItemImage id="ae2:wireless_booster" />
</Row>

By default, the tower covers a **1x1 chunk** area centered on itself. For every **16 Wireless Boosters** installed, one additional ring of chunks is added around it:

| Wireless Boosters | Horizontal range |
| ---: | :--- |
| 0～15 | 1x1 chunk |
| 16～31 | 3x3 chunks |
| 32～47 | 5x5 chunks |
| 48～63 | 7x7 chunks |
| 64 | 9x9 chunks |

The vertical range extends **128 blocks downward** and **256 blocks upward** from the tower base, limited by world height.

Expanding the range also increases standby power consumption. By default, a 1x1 chunk range consumes **4 AE/t**, and for each additional chunk within the range, an additional **8 AE/t** is consumed.
